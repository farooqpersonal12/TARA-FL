Most important rule — four baselines
We maintain four separate experimental baselines. This is directly aligned with the comparison
structure in the briefing, and it is what lets us attribute any improvement to a specific component.
 A
Normal FL
FedAvg
 B
Trust-based FL
Trust + FedAvg
 C
Robust FL
Trust + Fixed Robust Aggregation
 D
TARA-FL
Trust + Risk + Adaptive Aggregation
That way, when we eventually get a result such as the following, we can actually determine which
component contributed to the improvement, rather than simply claiming that TARA-FL is better:


=== PAGE 11 ===
TARA-FL Implementation Roadmap
Page 11
FedAvg      →  X%
Trust + FedAvg  →  Y%
Trust + Robust  →  Z%
TARA-FL     →  W%
In short: because trust, robust aggregation, and adaptive risk are layered on one at a time from
baseline A through D, the gap between any two rows isolates the contribution of the single component
added between them. That component-level attribution — not one headline accuracy number — is the
entire point of the study, and the reason baselines A, B, and C must never be modified once frozen.
